Session 9 — The Campaign 1 Finale

Six dream sequences, then Tower Dalmore. The crew has spent eight sessions setting up these dominoes. Tonight they fall. This session is deliberately more fixed than any prior one — the top-level outcome (the ritual completes, all six towers come down) does not change no matter what the crew does. What genuinely stays open: how the dreams resolve, how the ritual happens, who pays what cost, and whether Scurlock survives. See "The Design Principle" below before running this.

Before You Start

The Design Principle

The crew is Tier 0/1 — not powerful, just resourceful. Scurlock is effectively a Tier 3 threat acting entirely alone, no gang or underlings to chip away at — if anyone charges him physically, he simply body-checks them. Setarra is an actual demon; per this setting's own established genre rules (which the players already know), demons are not meant to be fought directly — the only ways to beat one are to avoid her or redirect her interest elsewhere. Season 2 is built around finding those external levers, because the crew could never take her on their own.

Given that, the crew's attempts to interfere tonight will have real narrative weight but little to no effect on the fixed outcome — except one specific gambit (killing Harker), which genuinely can change something. See "The One Real Lever" below.

The technique throughout: when a player tries something clever to stop a fixed beat, the answer is almost never "it doesn't work" delivered flatly. It's "it works, and here's the cost" or "here's the narratively consistent reason this specific threat doesn't bend" — reinforcing things already established about Scurlock and Setarra all campaign, not GM fiat in the moment.

Corrected geography: Scurlock has no tower of his own. His mansion sits in the shadow of Tower Dalmore itself — the tower from Session 8, bearing the Dalmorre crest, is Tower Dalmore. This matters at the end: when the towers fall, his home is directly in the collapse zone.

The Dream Card Game — Shared Rules

Applies to all six dreams. Player-facing version is on the printed handouts — this is the full reference.

Roles & Structure

5 non-dreaming players split every dream: 2 Helpers (red suits, Ace/2/3 each), 2 Hinderers (black suits, Ace/2/3 each), 1 City (rotates — one seat per player across the six dreams; holds 1 Black King + 1 Red King, not tied to rounds).

Exactly 3 gameplay rounds per dream — everything else is free roleplay/narration. Each round, every non-City player must either impact the dream (play a card face-down proposing an effect) or interrupt (play a card to contest an existing play, triggering a Showdown). No passing.

Showdown: flip both cards. 2 beats Ace, 3 beats 2, Ace beats 3. Exact tie → initiator wins (keeps proposing effects competitive with blocking them). Cards are spent once played, win or lose — all plays reveal at round's end. Hands: 3 → 2 → 1 across the three rounds.

City's Kings: playable any time, on any single dispute, to flip that outcome — Red King forces Help-favorable, Black King forces Hinder-favorable. Each usable once. The City isn't on the party's side — genuinely undecided, same as the city-spirit itself.

The dots/obstacles split (Flint, Costya, Harrow, Alaric) reverses dream to dream — never assume a default. Brick and Victor use a wholly separate deck-pool mechanic instead of dots/obstacles/rolls entirely — mirror images of each other (Brick erodes automatically, Victor stagnates without help).

10-minute timer per dream — announce it up front. Each dream's timeout resolution is bespoke, not a shared formula — see that dream's own section below.

Running Order — Locked

Flint(A) → Alaric(B) → Brick(C) → Costya(A) → Harrow(B) → Victor(C). Pattern A = Helpers add dots/Hinderers add obstacles. Pattern B = Helpers change the dream/Hinderers remove dots. Pattern C = no dice, deck draw only. Dreams 1-3 teach something new each time; 4-6 repeat the same three patterns so the table recognizes the shape instead of learning cold. Emotional pacing lines up too — Flint (heaviest grief) and Brick (heaviest agency-loss) aren't back to back; Costya (safest/lowest-stakes) lands right after Brick's gut-punch as a breather.

Dream 1 — Flint: "The Boiler"

Objective: reach the boiler valve before it's too late. Frame: genuinely winnable, not scripted-fixed — but even a full win gets revealed as false.

Setup: Flint as a child outside the Dalmorrian Boiler Works. Jared stumbles out drunk, ruffles his hair: "There's my favorite little shadow. Your folks in there? Tell 'em Jared says the boiler's fine — I checked it myself." Then shouting, smoke. His parents rush the boiler. He runs in after them.

Continuity dependency: assumes Flint has read the newspaper clipping (currently held by Harrow) before this dream — during the Session 9 opening roleplay.

Mechanics

No dots. Every roll: roll twice, take the lower. Helpers: add dots (cancels the disadvantage to a normal roll). Hinderers: add obstacles (smoke/crowd/geography lying). Hinderers cannot remove dots — he has none.

3 rounds: 1 The Crowd (pushing through fleeing workers) → 2 Wading (dream-logic floor resistance) → 3 The Valve.

Payoff

Fails: nothing but smoke and panic. Partial: the haze moves wrong, like reaching hands — unsettling, unexplained. Full success: he "saves them" — warm, relief, let it sit for a real beat — then it fades. Zoom out: alone on the floor in front of the ruptured boiler, dust swirling. It was never real. Never name what the dust is — no "Setarra," just dust. Let sharp players connect it to the newspaper's "ancient-seeming dark mineral dust" on their own.

Timer runs out: no scored tier — GM improv tied to exactly where he is. Crowd: "Even if I had tried, I would not have been able to find them." The Valve: "Even if I had gotten to the lock, I would not have been able to unlock it in time."

Dream 2 — Alaric: "The Founding"

Objective: complete the ritual successfully. Frame: a passenger in his ancestor's body at the founding of the Harbor Compact. No script fed to his player for his own lines.

Beat order (restructured): Beat 0 chatter (free improv, other 5 houses in character) → Round 1 (holding his presence) → Beat 1 The Calling (fixed, together): "We call upon the Watcher, by the authority of the Emperor, for the keeping of this city and the water that surrounds it." → Beat 2 the other 5 houses' vows (fixed; his own vow deliberately withheld — the ceremony waits him out, other houses react in character to the pause) → Round 2 (the weight of being watched) → Beat 3 The Terms (fixed): "...should any house's line fail, its portion falls to the five remaining, until none remain able to bear it." → Beat 4 The Binding (fixed): "Let this be witnessed. Let this be written. Let this be bound in water, which does not forget." → Round 3 his blood offering, the climax → Beat 5 The Watcher's Answer (visibility gated by desync).

The Twist & Mechanics

Each round needs one correct attribute (Insight/Prowess/Resolve — any skill under it counts). He doesn't know which. Wrong attribute doesn't fill "Hold the Face" regardless of dice.

  • Round 1 correct: Insight (reading/blending). Tempting-wrong: Resolve (actively charming the room).
  • Round 2 correct: Resolve (enduring by attuning). Tempting-wrong: Prowess (bracing like a fight).
  • Round 3 correct: Resolve/Attune (surrender, not control). Tempting-wrong: Prowess/Finesse (a controlled gesture). Helper hint flavor: "your blood is pounding right now."

Prowess is never correct, all three rounds — a real, discoverable pattern, don't lampshade it.

Helpers: add a hint toward next round's correct attribute. Hinderers: lower his dots directly.

Two clocks: "Hold the Face" (5 seg, good, correct rounds only, full=2/mixed=1/fail=0) vs "Not Supposed to Be Here" (6 seg, bad, flat +2 per wrong guess regardless of dice — the desync clock).

Payoff (by wrong-guess count)

0 wrong: full ceremony witnessed, lingering physical sensation carries into the finale (pressure/salt/ringing ears). 1 wrong: minor haze, a few details unrecoverable. 2 wrong: significant haze, retains the gist only. 3 wrong (full desync): Beat 5 is a blank — he was there and can't say how it ended. Play this as a real gap in memory, not just "missed intel."

Timer runs out: simply cut off, unfinished — never learns how it ends. Silence, not haze — a distinct outcome from the desync tiers.

Dream 3 — Brick: "The Replacement"

⚠ Pre-Session Prep Required

Get Brick's player to commit to exactly 4 distinct identity/memory pieces before tonight (marital status, a sibling, personal history, a name he'd answer to). Each becomes one physical red card. This dream can't run without that conversation happening first.

Objective: remember who you are — not his fight, everyone else's. Frame: zero agency, no dice, no choices. He suits up, drops endlessly. The Watcher: "I have watched so long. I would like, once, to be watched back. Stay. I will show you everything, if you let me see you the same way."

Mechanics — The Deck

Pool: 4 red (his memories) + 4 black added at the start of each round, cumulative, reshuffled fresh each round. Round-end: the Watcher asks "Who are you?" — draw once. Red = "I am Brick" (holds). Black = "I don't know" (fails). Odds: R1 ≈50%, R2 ≈33%, R3 =25% (worse if reds removed).

Helpers: grant advantage (draw 2, succeed on either red). Hinderers: permanently remove a red card — gone for the rest of the dream.

Payoff (by total failures, 0-3)

0: shaken but intact, +1 Beyond Takes Interest, aware why. 1: mild self-confusion. 2: misnames party/NPCs using the real player's name instead of the character's (e.g. "Ethan" instead of "Victor" — confirm correct real names before running: Zach=Flint, Ethan=Victor, Sean=Harrow, Andrew=Alaric). Advances Beyond Takes Interest harder. 3: believes he IS the diver — standing condition, new recovery clock, major step toward Beyond Takes Interest's endgame.

Timer runs out: unique — the dream doesn't end early. Add remaining rounds' black cards as normal, but table participation stops entirely (no more Helper/Hinderer cards) — Brick just draws cold, unaided, for whatever rounds are left.

Dream 4 — Costya: "The Last Car"

⚠ Pre-Session Prep Required

Shuffle Costya's skill-dot values across her sheet before tonight — the set of numbers stays the same, but reassign which skill holds which. She still sees the real sheet (knows the value distribution) but not the mapping. Applies to every roll she makes this dream.

Objective: follow the Conductor through the train, get him talking. Frame: not fixed history — a live conversation with the city-spirit's active agent; genuinely carries into the finale.

Setup: "The train cannot stop. The city cannot stop. Not on my match." Play the Conductor procedural, not mystical — catastrophe is administrative to him.

Mechanics & Structure

Cars 1/3/5 free (guaranteed dialogue, no roll). Cars 2/4/6 gated (Rounds 1/2/3). Dialogue delivers by a sequential success-counter, not literal car number — every successful contact (free stop or won round) increments the counter and delivers the next line in order. 3 guaranteed, up to 6 total.

Helpers: add dots. Hinderers: add obstacles (door seals, lurch, drowned passengers blocking the aisle). No dot-removal.

The Six Lines (deliver in order by counter, not car number)

1: "You're wondering if I'm still dead. Wrong question. I was never the interesting part. I keep schedule." 2: "You think the city's just brick and men. It isn't. It listens... Lately it doesn't like what it hears." 3: "Someone's been making promises on the city's behalf. Without asking it." 4: "Far as the city's concerned, you're just his hands right now... Hasn't decided if you know better." 5: "It's not asking for loyalty. It's asking to not be lied to twice in one lifetime." 6 (best-case only): "You're not just riding anymore. It knows your step on these boards. That's not nothing."

Payoff

Counter 3: unease only, city's default reading (extension of Scurlock) holds. 4-5: enough to understand the city is sentient and undecided. 6: clearest signal breaking from Scurlock is a real, open option — hers to carry back to the group.

Timer runs out: clean scripted ejection regardless of progress — the door opens, the Conductor: "This is your stop," ushers her off. No extra info. Score normally against whatever counter she'd reached.

Dream 5 — Harrow: "Same Time Next Week"

Objective: get out of the room of cultists. Frame: a trap dream. Escape is technically possible, but the space lies about its own geometry. Horror is politeness that won't stop, and an offer that isn't even a lie.

Setup: handler, warm: "Great work on the last task, Harrow. Same time next week." He turns to leave — the exit is gone, someone unrecognized stands there: "Hey Harrow! Where are you going?" Setarra, present but not yet acting.

The Twist & Mechanics

Bad clock — "The Circle Closes," 6 seg. Even a full success still ticks it (+1) — the better he does, the more the room reads him as belonging, not escaping. Scaling: full=+1, mixed=+2, fail=+3, crit maybe +0.

Helpers: create an opportunity (−1 clock fill). Hinderers: remove 1 dot directly. (Reversed split from Flint/Costya on purpose.)

Statue ladder (cultists move only when unwatched, tied to clock segments): 0-1 background/still. 2-3 close distance when unwatched, still freeze when watched. 4-5 move even under watch, still snap back. 6: no longer freeze at all, walking/crawling, closing in.

The Lingering Trap & the Morrow Tell

Standing temptation, any time, not tied to rounds: a menu of half-recognized faces he can try to place — always costs +1 (or a ladder jump), never resolves cleanly. Nell Vask (real contact, scars/dry humor). Pale Jeren (real contact, dust-rubbing). The Handless Clerk (punishes instead — shifts/vanishes mid-look). Veleris Quill (never met, feels like he should recognize her anyway — most unsettling). Never resolve whether it's his memory failing or something changed.

Morrow tell — separate, GM's timing, ideally mid-loop: total instant recognition, opposite texture. Brother Morrow, who never smiles, smiling. "You always did such careful work, Harrow. We kept a place for you. We still do." No threat in it — that's what makes it worse.

Payoff

6: hands finish closing, wakes calm, dust on collar. 3-5: free but rattled, Morrow's smile lingers unexplained. 0-2: clean break, then Setarra's voice, close and fond (live improv off the player's reaction) — unsettles more than a threat would.

Timer runs out: binary on trajectory. Still stuck → simply trapped. Trending toward success → believes he escaped, then hands on his back: "You REALLY thought you could get away?" — ends there.

Dream 6 — Victor: "The Circuit"

Objective: determine "The Pattern." Frame: no dice, no agency — inverted from Brick's. Fell asleep studying Dirmot's ley-line papers; the dream lets him walk the lines instead of just reading them, carried by a current he didn't choose.

The physical prop: a hidden ley-line map drawn on paper, covering a real map of Doskvol. Players never told what it represents ahead of time. Each successful round physically reveals a section. The table may piece it together faster than Victor's player does — that's fine, the goal is for the character to figure it out.

Mechanics — The Deck (inverted from Brick's)

Pool starts 1 red + 4 black (20%). Nothing changes automatically — no decay, but also no progress without help.

Helpers: permanently add 1 red card. Hinderers: apply disadvantage on that round's draw only (draw 2, need both red) — flavor: Dirmot's marginalia "talking back," cocky and needling.

The Three Reveals

Round 1: the papers are a literal subterranean map of "ghost field energy," flowing like rivers. The old Dalmorre tower's spirit well has converged under the new Dalmorrian residence.

Round 2: Strangford's own old tower shows the same pattern — drained into the modern Strangford mansion. But the heron house's old tower diverted and spread across several waterways/Gondolier territory instead of converging — mirrors the heron bloodline itself being scattered across many Gondoliers rather than concentrated.

Round 3 (hardest, final): real structural weak points where the city meets water with no leyline coverage — fragile, near collapse. Deeper: the six towers themselves shouldn't be able to stand — held up by this energy, not architecture. Plant for the finale climax: when the contract is revoked, all six towers come down, including the one Scurlock's own mansion sits in the shadow of. Line to land close to verbatim: "This city is built on a deck of cards — if things shift, there will be a physical fallout."

Payoff (by reveal count, 0-3)

0: nothing solid, possible harm from blowback. 1: Dalmorre shift only. 2: all convergences confirmed, structural danger missed entirely. 3: full picture — the most actionable, most alarming intel of any of the six dreams.

Timer runs out: visual frame — Victor's on a spectral gondola, loses steering entirely, current goes out of control, just tries to hold on. Table participation stops; remaining draws are automatic disadvantage (need both red). Fixed ending regardless: hits a bend, thrown overboard into turbulent water, sinks feeling the city collapsing around him — a raw, sensory version of the Round 3 reveal, delivered as feeling rather than earned understanding.

Arrival — Beats 1-3

The crew arrives at Tower Dalmore with the legal document from Session 8. Scurlock meets them, takes the document, and is direct: they may stay, but they must trust him no matter what happens, and they must not interfere. "If we are to stop Setarra, things must proceed exactly to my plan."

He ushers them to a separated, "safer" area before he begins — this is what makes the later cultist-wall (Beat 9) and general physical distance from the ritual plausible throughout.

The Bargain — Beats 4-8

Beat 4 — The Summons

Scurlock calls out: "I have come to fulfill my bargain." (Red flag, deliberately.)

Counter-play — If the crew moves to stop him here

Setarra's arrival becomes the deterrent on its own — the threat in the room shifts from "a man we could maybe grapple with" to "an actual demon standing right there," which naturally de-escalates through genre-savvy instinct.

If that's not enough: Scurlock breaks focus for a moment and appeals directly — "I know this is a lot, but please trust me." The player rolls (their choice — an Insight-style read on him, or a Resolve check on holding their own nerve) and the roll confirms he is genuinely sincere. Not lying, not manipulating — he truly believes this will work. Consider letting the roll also surface that he's afraid of the cost and doing it anyway; that reframes standing down as loyalty to a character they've grown attached to, not just tactical trust.

If they still push past that: Scurlock summons a ghost-field barrier, sealing the "safer area" off from the ritual zone entirely. Non-violent, absolute, consistent with a man capable of "certain arrangements" that have extended his life for centuries.

Beats 5-6 — Setarra Arrives, the Offering, the Addendum

Setarra asks if he's done what he said he would. He says yes — then adds, "I have an addendum." She scoffs, bites anyway: "What is it you want?" He offers her all the ritual components, provided the crew is not harmed. She laughs — "Scurly, you can't be serious!" — he: "I am serious. And don't call me Scurly." (Airplane! reference, played straight — let the table laugh, it's a real beat of levity right before things turn irreversible.) She agrees, provided the crew doesn't get in her way.

Counter-play — If the crew charges in during Beats 5-6

Setarra is focused on Scurlock and will not be stopped by the party — she does not negotiate with them or even address them directly; they are beneath her notice. Anyone who rushes her or Scurlock finds a dusty hand materializing to hold them back — effortless restraint, not violence, takes real time to shake off.

If other players try to help the restrained one, Scurlock notices and gets upset, imploring Setarra not to hurt them — this can be played as directly prompting his addendum above, rather than something he'd already planned regardless. Real weight for a "failed" interference: it changed the shape of the negotiation, even though the outcome didn't change.

Once agreed, she disdainfully flings the restrained party member backward — they fall, and she says she'll leave them alone. Humiliating, not harmful.

Beat 7 — The Release

She accepts his offering and releases him from his contract — reaches out, unpins his cloak. It drops heavily, releasing a massive cloud of dust that's been clinging to him. He gasps, breathing freely for the first time in a very long time.

Beat 8 — Scurlock Leaves

He turns to the crew, can't meet their gaze. "I... I am sorry. But there is no other way." He leaves quickly. Only the crew and Setarra remain in the tower. This is the branch point — any player(s) may choose to follow him now. See "If They Follow Scurlock" below for what happens to those who do.

The Room Fills — Beat 9

Ash Compact attendants flood in — some recognizable from Harrow's dream sequence this session. They block the party from ready access to whatever follows.

Counter-play — If the crew fights the cultists

A real, uphill fight — if they beat one, two more fill the gap. If they lose, they get overwhelmed and restrained, and Setarra forces them to "watch what is to come." This is also how Beat 9 is enforced mechanically for anyone who doesn't choose the assassination gambit below — losing this fight is what puts them in position to witness Beats 10-12 regardless of intent.

The Second Bargain — Beats 10-12

Beat 10 — The Reveal

Setarra turns to the room, beaming: "I have come to accept a deal!" A hooded figure steps forward: "And I have come to offer one." Throws back the hood — it's Harker.

Counter-play — Alaric's cry (expected)

Expect Alaric's player to cry out and warn Harker. Setarra is expecting this too — she shoves a dusty hand down his throat to silence him. This opens the progress clock "Alaric Suffocates."

Harm tiers: Level 1 — Partially suffocated (can't speak, can still try to help himself). Level 2 — Pass out (unconscious; can no longer act for himself, but the rest of the party can still work to save him). Level 3 — Severe oxygen deprivation.

Clearing it becomes an immediate party priority — open to whatever skill fits the fictional approach a player proposes (Attune to magically clear the airway, Finesse to physically dig the dust out, etc.).

Harker is shocked, then delighted — this is the first time he's had a hard time finding something to say to Alaric. He reveals he's always hated Alaric, resents him for what he has — and then the gut-punch: "Did you know, Alaric, that YOUR father caused all of this?" Reveals Schieven's deal with Setarra that ruined Jared decades ago (see Session 8 recap / the finale knowledge doc). Alaric cannot respond — he's still choking. Once the clock clears, give this its own real beat before returning to business as usual; it reshapes his relationship with his father going into Season 2.

Beat 11 — The Renegotiation

Setarra and Harker renegotiate the Harbor Compact — she's renamed sole defender of the city against "the horrors." The accountable houses' future payment terms are left deliberately vague (hers to redefine later). Harker states he's ready to make good on his household's blood payment — one they never neglected. She asks by what authority. He claims inheritance: by blood, he can renegotiate, since he feels the Watcher failed to protect his family's fortunes, and if the Watcher could have stopped it and didn't, that's someone's fault.

Beat 12 — The Payment

She accepts, offers him the Tide Tithe as recompense — he greedily takes it. As he receives it, it violently erupts from every orifice and fills the open, casket-shaped Drowned Ledger — his blood payment made in full. Body horror: gold, notes, fish hooks, broken toys, everything ever offered to the Watcher, exiting his body. He falls into the casket dead. It snaps shut. The ritual ends.

The One Real Lever — Killing Harker

This is the single thing the crew could try that genuinely changes an outcome. Prior to any assassination attempt, Setarra is not actively protecting Harker — she doesn't consider the party a real threat and hasn't anticipated this specific move. That means there's a real, narrow window if the crew commits to reaching him (through the cultist gauntlet above) and lands a serious hit.

If they succeed — mortally wound him, not necessarily kill outright:

  • Setarra is genuinely surprised and furious — a real crack in her control, since she's otherwise thought of everything.
  • She immediately "applies pressure to the wound," compacting dust onto it to stop the bleeding and stabilize him. Because her attention shifts from Alaric to Harker in this moment, this can passively ease the "Alaric Suffocates" clock — one bold action indirectly saving a teammate.
  • She turns her full attention to the party: "Finish. The. Words." to Harker — and he keeps going regardless.
  • She may knock the attacker clear off the tower for the attempt — not dead, sidelined for the rest of the scene.
  • She then stares down the rest of the crew — opens a deliberately oversized clock, "Stare Down Setarra." Not winnable by design; its only function is to occupy and paralyze the party while the ritual concludes around them (same narrative role as being "forced to watch" after losing to the cultists). The cultists' own tone shifts here too — cocky and chanting becomes visibly fearful, now that she's turned her fury outward.
  • She locks down hard on protecting Harker after this — there is no second attempt.
  • The payoff: the ritual still completes and the season still ends the same way structurally (towers still fall) — but it's incomplete. Not all of Harker's blood reaches the ledger. Her claim on the compact is weaker than it should be. This is the Season 2 opening — a real, mechanically-grounded crack in her ownership the crew can contest later, consistent with how precise and legalistic this whole compact storyline has always been about exact terms and exact blood.

If they try and fail (don't reach him, or the strike doesn't land): treat as the standard cultist-gauntlet outcome — overwhelmed, restrained, forced to watch. No unique consequence beyond that.

Aftermath — Beats 13-14

Setarra laughs in victory and disappears in a cloud of dust with the Ledger. The cultists — who have been chanting, jockeying with the party, observing throughout (consider some call-and-response chanting for atmosphere) — scatter the moment she and the reliquary vanish.

Things shift. Tower Dalmore begins to fall. The party rolls to escape unharmed — a real, mechanically meaningful moment of agency, even though the outcome around them is fixed. All six towers collapse. A distant, muffled roar. All the water in the city goes unnaturally still.

If They Follow Scurlock — Beats 15-16

They catch up to him sitting on the edge of his mansion's roof, feet dangling, the dilapidated Tower Dalmore looming overhead. He's contemplative — reflecting on his relationship with the city, acknowledging things look bad now but "things will shake out in a century or two."

Whoever follows will likely demand to know what he's thinking, and may turn violent, or try social skills to dissuade him. His fate is the one genuinely variable outcome in the whole finale — entirely shaped by how they engage him. Three branches:

Outcome 1 — Compassionate approach → He dies

He's resigned to his fate, defeated, depressed. The crew tries to shake him out of it and fails — he no longer has any fight left in him for what's coming with Setarra. The tower collapses onto him and his mansion. He looks surprised at first, then: "...yeah, figures." Confirmed dead entering Season 2.

Outcome 2 — Confrontational-but-not-hostile → Survives, absent

He's engaged but defensive — paces, insists this is all part of the plan, looks for approval and doesn't get it. The towers begin to collapse; he's shocked, backs away, and runs. His fate is unknown to the party at the end of the finale. He does survive — a potential ally for Season 2, but the obstacle is finding him and convincing him to help fix what he helped cause. Real recruitment-arc material, not a guaranteed alliance.

Outcome 3 — Hostile approach → Survives, driven, likely doomed

He gets angry: "I know best. The city will survive, it always has. Who do you think you are? I KNOW WHAT I AM DOING." If only one player followed, he holds them by the throat over the roof's edge, still arguing his case. If a second is present, he's knocked clear to the other end of the roof.

Then the tower begins to collapse. The player he's holding watches a rapid shift across his face — shock, disbelief, fury, horror. He drops them. As they fall, they see the tower crash onto his mansion and hear him scream "What have I done? WHAT HAVE I DONE?!?" They black out.

The falling player's fate is a genuine cliffhanger — unresolved for the whole table that night. Talk to that specific player privately before Season 2 begins; they decide whether their character survived. Scurlock himself does survive this branch — the party won't know that at the finale's end, but he's now so angry, guilty, and fully bought-in to destroying Setarra that he probably won't survive the end of Season 2. Driven, reckless, racing toward his own likely sacrifice.

Season's End — Beat 17

The season ends with the dust of the collapsed towers settling over the whole city, extending as far as Brightstone, as Setarra's laughter echoes. Then all is still.

Quick Reference

  • Fixed no matter what: the ritual completes, all six towers fall, Setarra escapes with the Ledger.
  • Genuinely variable: whether Harker's payment is complete (only via the assassination gambit — Season 2 opening if they land it), and Scurlock's fate (entirely down to how his followers engage him).
  • Setarra never addresses the party directly except the one dismissive line to anyone leaving with Scurlock, and if the assassination attempt lands. She considers them beneath her until they force her attention.
  • "Stare Down Setarra" is a paralysis device, not a winnable contest — its only job is to hold the party in place while the ritual finishes.
